# How Part B is marked (40 marks)

**About 5 of the 40 marks depend directly on how good the model is.** The other 35 reward the problem framing, the stakeholder analysis, creative methods, novelty and delivery. A modest model with a sharp story beats a strong model with a vague one.

| Criterion | Sub-criterion | Marks | What markers look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem and stakeholder | Clarity and relevance | 5 | The tactile problem is clearly defined, and why touch helps (where vision alone fails) is argued well |
| Problem and stakeholder | Stakeholder analysis | 5 | The stakeholder's needs, objectives and potential impact are understood and presented concisely |
| Proposed solution | Feasibility | 5 | Methods, models, datasets and metrics are realistic and suit the problem; risks are recognised; the proof of concept supports the plan |
| Proposed solution | Creativity and innovation | 5 | Novelty in the methods: architecture, learning strategy, sensing combination or evaluation. A new dataset alone earns little. |
| Novelty | Open | 10 | Originality of the overall idea, framing or insight, clearly positioned against the papers it builds on and against the obvious approach |
| Presentation | Clarity and structure | 5 | Well organised, flows logically, keeps to 5:00 |
| Presentation | Engagement and delivery | 5 | Engaging, confident and professional; every member presents; it works as one video, not six talks |

## What each criterion asks of us

**Problem and stakeholder (10).**
- State the problem in one sentence the stakeholder would agree with.
- Say why touch helps where a camera does not (occlusion inside a grasp, poor lighting, properties you cannot see such as hardness).
- Answer three questions: what does the stakeholder need, what are they trying to achieve, and what changes for them if it is solved? Say what "good enough" means for them: accuracy, speed, safety or cost.

**Proposed solution (10).**
- Explain why deep learning fits, which data we would use and how it would be labelled, how we would measure success, and what could go wrong.
- Show the proof of concept: our test result against the reference point, the demo, and one failure case. Say honestly what it does and does not show.

**Novelty (10).**
- What do the papers we build on already do, and what do we add?
- Why is this not just a standard model applied to a standard problem?
- Back the claim with evidence where we can, for example a result the obvious approach does not reach, or a failure case it does not handle.

**Presentation (10).**
- Storyboard first, then script and time it: about 130 to 150 words a minute, so roughly 100 to 110 words each for six speakers.
- One slide template, one colour scheme, planned hand-overs. Show real tactile images early: most viewers have never seen one.

## The peer factor

Each of us gets `Part B mark x peer factor`.

- Everyone rates everyone, including themselves, 1 to 5 on five criteria. Peer assessment closes **Wed 23 Dec, 23:59**.
- Peer factor = your peer score divided by the group average.
- Between 0.90 and 1.10 it is set to exactly 1. Otherwise it is kept between 0.50 and 1.10, and the Part B mark after scaling never exceeds 40.
- Documented non-contribution can take it below 0.50. The module team checks every adjustment against our charter, minutes and contribution logs.

So the records in this repo are not bureaucracy: they are the evidence that protects each of us.

## Late work

5 marks lost per day late, for up to 7 days, then zero. Late Part B files cost the whole group.
